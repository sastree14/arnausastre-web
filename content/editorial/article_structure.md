# Article Structure

## Title

Follow one of these patterns. Adapt to the topic — do not copy literally.

- "Why most X fail in practice"
- "The hidden cost of Y"
- "What Z actually requires"
- "How companies approach X — and why it matters"
- "The difference between X and Y in practice"
- "What X tells you that Y cannot"
- "Building Z that companies actually use"

The title must make a specific claim or promise. Vague titles are not acceptable.

Bad: "The Importance of Demand Forecasting"
Good: "Why demand forecasting fails before the algorithm is even chosen"

## Excerpt

1–2 sentences. Direct and specific. No vague generalities. No "in this article we will explore".

Bad: "In this article, we explore the importance of forecasting for retail businesses."
Good: "Most retail demand forecasting failures happen before any model is trained. This article explains why, and what to do instead."

## Body structure

### Subtitles

Each section starts with a short **bold** subtitle followed by 1–2 paragraphs.
This is lightweight markdown — not MDX, not HTML.
Bold subtitles only. No heading levels (no ##, no ###).

### Opening section

The first section must make a substantive point immediately.
It must not restate the title, summarise the article, or describe what will be discussed.

Bad opening: "Demand forecasting is a critical capability for any retail organisation. In this article..."
Good opening: "**The problem is not the algorithm**\nMost forecasting projects fail during data preparation, not model selection. The choice between ARIMA and gradient boosting is irrelevant if the input data conflates returns with sales."

### Body sections

Prefer:
- Contrast-driven reasoning: "Most systems focus on A. The real challenge is B."
- Concrete examples drawn from the research, not invented scenarios
- Specific trade-offs, not generic best practices
- Statements that a reader could disagree with — not truisms

Avoid:
- Sections that only describe what will be discussed
- Lists of obvious points that add no analytical insight
- Conclusions that merely summarise what was already said

### Closing

End with a concrete implication or decision framework.
Not a summary. Not a call to action. A final insight that reframes the reader's understanding.
