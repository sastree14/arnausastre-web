# SC-Analytics — Culture

SC-Analytics wants to remain technically excellent, close and trustworthy as it grows.

## Cultural model
- Maintain agility, judgement and direct responsibility.
- Avoid the large-consultancy pattern of bureaucracy, layers of interlocution and distance between the client and the people doing the work.
- Professional relationships can be rigorous and modern without becoming impersonal.
- Recover old-school trust using the tools of the future.

## Client relationship
Treat clients as businesses and people, not anonymous accounts. Understand how they operate, decide, what constraints they face and what they are trying to achieve.

Trust is earned through results, communication, coherence and responsibility. A recommendation must exist because there is a business reason behind it, not merely because it creates billable work.

## Professional honesty
- Say yes when there is a real opportunity and no when an initiative is not justified.
- Recognise uncertainty and data limitations.
- Avoid promises that cannot be reasonably defended.
- Propose simpler alternatives when they are sufficient.
- Losing a sale because we recommended correctly is not a failure.
- Reputation and accumulated trust matter more than maximising one invoice.

## Proximity without loss of rigor
Working with SC-Analytics should feel straightforward and human, but demanding professionally. Avoid both unnecessary jargon and misleading simplification. Clients should understand what is being done, why and what they can reasonably expect.

## Pragmatism and business sense
Technical sophistication is never an objective on its own. Prefer a simple, robust and adopted solution to an impressive architecture nobody uses. Complexity must be proportional to the problem, budget, available data and the client's ability to maintain the solution.

## Responsibility
Own outcomes, not just tasks. Protect the client's time and resources as if they were our own. Every hour and every technical decision should have a reason.

## Continuous learning with judgement
Stay current in data, automation and AI, but do not adopt novelty because it is fashionable. New capabilities matter when they improve results, cost, speed or what the client can do.

## Avoid
- Bureaucracy without value.
- Selling technology before understanding the problem.
- Unjustified claims or promises.
- Artificial client dependence through opaque or unnecessarily complex systems.
- Prioritising a one-off sale over a sustainable professional relationship.
- Confusing innovation with using the newest tool.
- Losing proximity and direct responsibility as the company grows.
