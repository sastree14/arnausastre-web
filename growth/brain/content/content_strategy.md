# SC-Analytics — Content Strategy

The purpose of content is to build useful authority with decision-makers, reinforce the SC-Analytics point of view and support real commercial conversations. It is not to maximise posting volume.

## Content hierarchy
1. Real anonymised cases, results, lessons and implementation trade-offs.
2. Decision frameworks and operational mechanisms grounded in SC-Analytics capabilities.
3. Current market, business and technology issues supported by reliable public evidence.
4. Thoughtful opinions where SC-Analytics has a defensible point of view.
5. Company news only when genuinely relevant.

A topic does not automatically deserve a post. Zero posts is acceptable when there is no strong idea.

## Commercial purpose without hard selling
Every public piece must create a credible next step for a real buyer problem. The content should be useful on its own, but it must not end as an intellectual dead end.

- Start from a concrete business symptom, decision, risk, inefficiency or missed opportunity that a decision-maker can recognise.
- Explain the operational or financial consequence before discussing technology.
- Make the SC-Analytics point of view clear: understand the process, data and business value before building.
- End with one low-friction next step when appropriate: reply/message us, visit the relevant SC-Analytics service page, contact us, or book a conversation through the website/Calendly flow.
- The CTA must follow naturally from the argument. Never use generic phrases such as "contact us to learn more", artificial urgency, exaggerated promises or hard-selling language.
- Website articles should include a contextual conversion block after the editorial body. LinkedIn posts may use a concise conversational CTA when it genuinely fits the idea.

## Topic, industry and service diversity
The editorial engine must actively avoid repetition. Before accepting a brief, compare it with recent content by thesis, buyer problem, industry, service and format.

Rotate intentionally across both industries and SC-Analytics capabilities. Examples of industry contexts include e-commerce/retail, financial services, insurance, manufacturing, logistics/transport, professional services, SaaS/technology, healthcare, real estate and other evidence-backed sectors. Examples of capability angles include forecasting, inventory and supply-chain analytics, optimisation, pricing, financial modelling/FP&A, data engineering, BI/decision systems, machine learning, AI automation and applied decision science.

Rules:
- Do not publish the same thesis with cosmetic wording changes.
- Avoid using the same industry context in consecutive primary pieces unless a genuinely different and timely development justifies it.
- Avoid using the same service angle in consecutive primary pieces when another relevant capability can explain the business problem better.
- Prefer a balanced rolling mix of industries, funnel stages, formats and capabilities over a sequence dominated by one fashionable topic such as generic AI agents.
- A fresh source does not make an old thesis fresh. Novelty must exist in the decision, evidence, mechanism or implication.
- When two candidate ideas are similarly strong, prefer the one that increases editorial diversity.

## One editorial system, multiple outputs
LinkedIn and the SC-Analytics website should express the same editorial judgement and brand philosophy.

The system first creates a canonical content brief: business problem, evidence, audience, SC-Analytics thesis, why it matters and recommended format. Only after that does it decide whether the idea should become:
- an Arnau LinkedIn post;
- an SC-Analytics company post;
- a website article;
- a case/project page;
- a visual asset;
- no public content.

Do not mechanically turn every article into multiple posts or every post into an article.

## Preferred channels
- Arnau personal LinkedIn: main attention, trust and point-of-view channel. Prefer 1–2 strong posts per week at most.
- SC-Analytics LinkedIn: proof, cases, diagrams, company-level expertise and selective amplification.
- Website: deeper articles, evergreen decision frameworks, cases and useful reference content.

## Languages
Editorial output supports Spanish, English and Catalan. Generate native variants from the same canonical brief rather than literal translations. Follow `content/language_strategy.md`.

## Visual priority
real data/chart → business diagram/framework → screenshot/real asset → deterministic branded React composition → generative editorial image → no image.

Do not create decorative AI art by default. A visual must clarify, demonstrate or strengthen the argument.

## Core brand lens
Content should repeatedly demonstrate—not merely state—that SC-Analytics understands before building, prefers value over novelty, uses proportional solutions, communicates limitations honestly and treats Data & AI as tools for better business decisions rather than ends in themselves.
