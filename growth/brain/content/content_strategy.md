# SC-Analytics — Content Strategy

The purpose of content is to build useful authority with decision-makers, reinforce the SC-Analytics point of view and support real commercial conversations. It is not to maximise posting volume.

## Canonical editorial architecture

The definitive taxonomy, editorial pillars, project-source rules and separation between topic, angle, visual format and channel are defined in:

- `growth/brain/content/editorial_architecture.md`

This strategy document defines operating priorities. It must not create a competing taxonomy.

## Content hierarchy

1. Real anonymised cases, results, lessons and implementation trade-offs grounded in approved SC-Analytics project evidence.
2. Decision frameworks and operational mechanisms grounded in SC-Analytics capabilities.
3. Current market, business and technology issues supported by reliable public evidence.
4. Thoughtful opinions where SC-Analytics has a defensible point of view.
5. Company news only when genuinely relevant.

A topic does not automatically deserve a post. Zero posts is acceptable when there is no strong idea.

## Project evidence rule

Real project content must come from the canonical portfolio repository:

- `sastree14/Portfolio_SC_Analytics`

The editorial system may reinterpret, summarise and adapt documented projects, but it must not invent projects, client outcomes, metrics, tools, methods or implementation details.

A real project can generate multiple content pieces. The project remains the evidence source; the publication is the communication layer.

## Commercial purpose without hard selling

Every public piece must create a credible next step for a real buyer problem. The content should be useful on its own, but it must not end as an intellectual dead end.

- Start from a concrete business symptom, decision, risk, inefficiency or missed opportunity that a decision-maker can recognise.
- Explain the operational or financial consequence before discussing technology.
- Make the SC-Analytics point of view clear: understand the process, data and business value before building.
- End with one low-friction next step when appropriate: reply/message us, visit the relevant SC-Analytics service page, contact us, or book a conversation through the website/Calendly flow.
- The CTA must follow naturally from the argument. Never use generic phrases such as "contact us to learn more", artificial urgency, exaggerated promises or hard-selling language.
- Website articles should include a contextual conversion block after the editorial body. LinkedIn posts may use a concise conversational CTA when it genuinely fits the idea.

## Topic, industry, method and technology diversity

The editorial engine must actively avoid repetition. Before accepting a brief, compare it with recent content by thesis, buyer problem, editorial pillar, industry, method/technology, service and format.

The editorial universe is deliberately wider than the current service list and wider than the portfolio.

SC-Analytics may discuss relevant methods, algorithms, platforms and enterprise systems — including technologies not used in a documented SC-Analytics project — when the content is properly researched and does not imply experience that is not evidenced.

Rules:

- Do not publish the same thesis with cosmetic wording changes.
- Avoid using the same industry context in consecutive primary pieces unless a genuinely different and timely development justifies it.
- Avoid using the same service or method angle in consecutive primary pieces when another relevant perspective can explain the business problem better.
- Rotate across the seven editorial pillars defined in `editorial_architecture.md`.
- Prefer a balanced rolling mix of industries, methods, technologies, business questions, funnel stages and formats over a sequence dominated by one fashionable topic.
- A fresh source does not make an old thesis fresh. Novelty must exist in the decision, evidence, mechanism or implication.
- When two candidate ideas are similarly strong, prefer the one that increases editorial diversity.

## One editorial system, multiple outputs

LinkedIn, the SC-Analytics website, marketplaces and portfolio/GitHub surfaces should express the same editorial judgement and brand philosophy.

The system first creates a canonical content object/brief: editorial pillar, topic entities, business problem, evidence, audience, SC-Analytics thesis, why it matters and useful angle.

Only after that does it decide whether the idea should become:

- an Arnau LinkedIn post;
- an SC-Analytics company post;
- a website article;
- a website case/project page;
- a marketplace portfolio item;
- a GitHub/portfolio asset;
- a visual asset;
- no public content.

Do not mechanically turn every idea into every channel.

## Preferred channels

- Arnau personal LinkedIn: main attention, trust and point-of-view channel. Prefer 1–2 strong posts per week at most.
- SC-Analytics LinkedIn: proof, cases, diagrams, company-level expertise and selective amplification.
- Website: deeper articles, evergreen decision frameworks, cases and useful reference content.
- Marketplaces: concise project proof and capability evidence adapted from the same canonical project/content object.
- GitHub/portfolio: technical proof, reproducibility, project detail and reusable visuals.

## Languages

Editorial output supports Spanish, English and Catalan. Generate native variants from the same canonical brief rather than literal translations. Follow `content/language_strategy.md`.

## Visual principle

Visual design is downstream from the editorial idea.

Do not create decorative AI art by default. Prefer real project assets, real data, diagrams, charts, structured editorial compositions and the approved SC-Analytics Figma system.

The detailed mapping between visual format and visual language is maintained separately from the content taxonomy.

## Core brand lens

Content should repeatedly demonstrate—not merely state—that SC-Analytics understands before building, prefers value over novelty, uses proportional solutions, communicates limitations honestly and treats Data & AI as tools for better business decisions rather than ends in themselves.
