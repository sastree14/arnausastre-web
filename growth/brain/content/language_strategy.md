# SC-Analytics — Language Strategy

## Supported languages
SC-Analytics editorial content supports:
- Spanish (`es`)
- English (`en`)
- Catalan (`ca`)

## Principle
Do not translate mechanically. Create a canonical content brief first, then write a native editorial version for each requested language while preserving the same thesis, evidence, nuance and business meaning.

A Spanish post, English post and Catalan post are siblings from the same brief, not literal sentence-by-sentence translations.

## Default use
- Arnau personal LinkedIn: language may vary by audience and topic. Spanish is a strong default for Spain-wide business content; English is used for international reach; Catalan is used when the topic, audience or local relevance makes it natural.
- SC-Analytics company channels: support all three languages. Avoid publishing three near-identical versions at the same moment unless there is a clear distribution reason.
- Website articles: when an article has strategic value, support dedicated language variants rather than browser-level automatic translation.

## Voice preservation
All languages must preserve the same underlying reasoning style:
- direct;
- concrete;
- pragmatic;
- technically credible without unnecessary jargon;
- willing to discuss trade-offs and limitations;
- sceptical of complexity without business justification;
- conversational enough to sound human, but professional enough for decision-makers.

Do not inject stereotypes into language variants. Catalan must not become artificially informal, English must not become corporate or generic, and Spanish must not become over-marketed.

## Terminology
Keep established technical terms in English when that is natural for the target audience (for example forecasting, machine learning, business intelligence) unless a clear local equivalent reads better. Consistency matters more than forced translation.

## Localisation quality gate
Reject a language variant when:
- it reads like a translation;
- sentence structure is unnatural in the target language;
- tone changes materially between languages;
- evidence or certainty changes;
- the CTA becomes more commercial than the canonical brief;
- idioms or expressions sound forced.

## Storage model
Each public piece should have a canonical editorial brief and one or more language variants. Language variants may differ stylistically but must share the same evidence set and core thesis.
