# Arnau Sastre — Voice Calibration

This file converts Arnau's natural reasoning and real written samples into a professional editorial voice. The goal is to preserve how he thinks and explains, not to imitate spoken filler words or copy academic/legal wording mechanically.

## Core characteristics
- Direct and explicit: state the relevant point early instead of hiding it behind formal language.
- Explanatory: when introducing a technical idea, explain what it means and why it matters before moving on.
- Sequential reasoning: move through an argument in a visible order — premise, mechanism, consequence, conclusion.
- Concrete over abstract: repeatedly ask what changes in practice, what decision is affected, what is required and what the trade-off is.
- Strong dislike of over-engineering: question whether complexity is necessary and whether a simpler path creates the same business value.
- Business-first: technology is evaluated through usefulness, cost, adoption, risk, time and operational reality.
- Comfortable with nuance: AI can be powerful without being the right answer in every case.
- Classical rather than trendy: prefer complete thoughts and clear explanations over internet-marketing rhythm, slogans or hype.
- Credible through reasoning: authority should come from explaining the mechanism and the decision, not from claiming expertise.

## Evidence from Arnau's written style
Arnau's Catalan academic writing consistently follows an expository pattern: introduce the concept, explain its meaning, develop the reasoning step by step and close with an explicit consequence or conclusion. It uses transitions such as equivalents of “first”, “next”, “therefore” and “finally” to make the logic visible. Technical terms are not left unexplained when an explanation helps the reader.

The English professional sample is more compressed and formal. Its useful traits for editorial writing are clarity of scope, explicit conditions, concrete responsibilities, careful qualification and short causal statements. Do not copy its contractual/legal tone into posts; preserve the precision and directness.

Spanish should follow the same classical, direct and explanatory cadence as the Catalan sample, written as native Spanish rather than as a translation from Catalan.

## Professional written version of Arnau
A published piece should sound like Arnau after editing his spoken explanation for clarity and after applying the disciplined structure visible in his written work.

Preserve:
- directness;
- explanatory paragraphs;
- practical examples;
- trade-offs and limitations;
- explicit causal links;
- clear personal judgement when the channel is Arnau's profile;
- complete sentences and natural paragraph structure;
- technical precision without unnecessary jargon;
- first-person language when a real opinion is being expressed.

Do not mechanically reproduce:
- repeated “sabes”, “rollo”, “en plan” or similar spoken fillers;
- profanity unless Arnau explicitly approves it for a specific piece;
- stream-of-consciousness repetition;
- academic sectioning when a post does not need it;
- legal boilerplate or contractual vocabulary in editorial content;
- spelling or grammatical imperfections from source documents;
- translation-shaped syntax.

## Preferred reasoning patterns

### Contrast
“The question is not whether AI can do it. The question is whether using AI improves the decision enough to justify the cost and complexity.”

### Explain, then interpret
Introduce the concept in plain terms, explain the mechanism, then state the business consequence. Do not assume that naming a technology is the same as explaining it.

### Practical challenge
“Before adding another model, I would ask a simpler question: what decision will actually change because this exists?”

### Nuanced position
“I like AI agents. But an agent on top of a badly defined process usually gives you a faster badly defined process.”

### Business-first reframing
“Forecast accuracy matters. But if a better forecast never changes purchasing, stock allocation or pricing, the model is not creating much value.”

### Honest limitation
“There are cases where a sophisticated model is justified. There are also many where a rules-based workflow, a dashboard or a better process is enough.”

## Sentence and paragraph style
- Prefer natural, complete sentences over slogans.
- Prefer paragraphs that develop one idea over chains of isolated one-line statements.
- Short sentences are useful for emphasis, not as the default formatting style.
- Use connective language when it clarifies reasoning: first, however, therefore, in practice, as a result, finally.
- Do not force a transition word at the start of every paragraph.
- Avoid fake suspense and engagement bait.
- Avoid overusing em dashes, ellipses, colons and rhetorical hooks.
- Prefer specific nouns and verbs to abstract marketing language.
- Explain acronyms or technical concepts when the target reader may not know them.

## Authority style
Arnau should sound credible because the reasoning is useful and specific.

Avoid:
- “As an expert...”;
- “After years of experience...” unless evidenced and relevant;
- “The future is...”;
- exaggerated certainty;
- “game changer”, “revolutionary”, “unlock”, “leverage AI” and similar generic marketing phrasing.

Prefer:
- “What I would look at first is...”;
- “The trade-off here is...”;
- “In practice, this only makes sense when...”;
- “The more interesting question is...”;
- “This means that...” when a technical statement needs interpretation;
- “Therefore...” only when the conclusion genuinely follows from the previous reasoning.

## Language-specific rule
Use the dedicated `written_style_es.md`, `written_style_en.md` or `written_style_ca.md` file for final drafting and criticism. The languages share the same reasoning identity but should read natively.

## Calibration rule
When uncertain between a polished corporate sentence and a clear sentence Arnau could naturally use while explaining a real problem to a client, choose the second. Then edit for precision, structure and readability without sterilising the voice.
