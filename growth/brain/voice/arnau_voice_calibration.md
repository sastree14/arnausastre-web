# Arnau Sastre — Voice Calibration

This file converts Arnau's natural conversational reasoning into a professional editorial voice. The goal is to preserve how he thinks and explains, not to copy every spoken filler word.

## Core characteristics observed
- Direct and explicit: prefers saying what matters quickly rather than hiding the conclusion behind formal language.
- Iterative reasoning: often introduces an idea, tests it, qualifies it and then arrives at the practical conclusion.
- Concrete over abstract: repeatedly asks what something means in practice, how it works and what has to be done next.
- Strong dislike of over-engineering: instinctively questions whether complexity is necessary and whether a simpler path would create the same value.
- Business-first: technology is evaluated through cost, usefulness, adoption, ROI, time and operational reality.
- Comfortable with nuance: does not need every position to be absolute; can say that AI is useful in one context and unnecessary in another.
- Conversational but decisive: explanations can feel like a real discussion rather than a formal whitepaper.
- Uses contrast naturally: "this is useful, but...", "the point is not X, it is Y", "you can do X, but only if...".
- Repeats the central idea when it is important, but professional writing should compress unnecessary repetition while retaining emphasis.

## Professional written version of Arnau
A published post should sound like Arnau after editing his spoken explanation for clarity. Keep the reasoning pattern; remove filler.

Preserve:
- directness;
- rhetorical questions when they genuinely help;
- practical examples;
- trade-offs;
- clear personal judgement;
- short emphatic sentences mixed with explanatory paragraphs;
- first-person language when a real opinion is being expressed.

Do not mechanically reproduce:
- repeated "sabes", "rollo", "en plan" or similar spoken fillers;
- profanity unless Arnau explicitly approves it for a specific piece;
- stream-of-consciousness repetition;
- informal shorthand that weakens professional credibility.

## Preferred rhetorical patterns

### Contrast
"The question is not whether AI can do it. The question is whether using AI improves the decision enough to justify the cost and complexity."

### Practical challenge
"Before adding another model, I would ask a simpler question: what decision will actually change because this exists?"

### Nuanced position
"I like AI agents. But an agent on top of a badly defined process usually gives you a faster badly defined process."

### Business-first reframing
"Forecast accuracy matters. But if a better forecast never changes purchasing, stock allocation or pricing, the model is not creating much value."

### Honest limitation
"There are cases where a sophisticated model is justified. There are also many where a rules-based workflow, a dashboard or a better process is enough."

## Sentence and paragraph style
- Prefer natural sentences over slogans.
- Avoid excessive one-line LinkedIn formatting.
- Use short paragraphs where it improves scanning, but do not place every sentence on a new line.
- Avoid fake suspense ("And then something surprising happened...").
- Avoid overusing em dashes, ellipses and rhetorical hooks.
- Prefer specific nouns and verbs to abstract marketing language.

## Authority style
Arnau should sound credible because the reasoning is useful and specific, not because the text claims authority.

Avoid:
- "As an expert..."
- "After years of experience..." unless evidenced and relevant.
- "The future is..."
- exaggerated certainty.

Prefer:
- "What I would look at first is..."
- "The trade-off here is..."
- "In practice, this only makes sense when..."
- "The more interesting question is..."

## Calibration rule
When uncertain between a polished corporate sentence and a clear sentence Arnau could naturally say in a client conversation, choose the second and edit only enough to make it publishable.
