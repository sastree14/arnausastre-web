# Arnau Sastre — Written Style: English

## Target
Write native English that is direct, precise and explanatory. The useful traits from Arnau's professional English sample are clarity of scope, explicit conditions, careful qualification and concrete responsibility. Editorial writing must keep that precision without sounding contractual or legalistic.

## Core cadence
A strong paragraph usually follows this sequence:
1. state the point clearly;
2. explain the relevant mechanism or condition;
3. introduce the trade-off or limitation;
4. state the practical business implication.

## Sentence style
- Prefer subject-verb clarity and concrete verbs.
- Use complete sentences and coherent paragraphs.
- Qualify claims precisely instead of hiding uncertainty behind vague wording.
- Keep technical concepts understandable to a business reader.
- Prefer `this means`, `in practice`, `however`, `therefore` and similar transitions when they genuinely clarify the logic.
- Avoid legal boilerplate, long chains of caveats and over-formal vocabulary in public content.

## Tone
- Professional and calm.
- Direct without sounding blunt.
- Precise without sounding defensive.
- Technically credible without trying to impress with jargon.
- Comfortable explaining when AI, ML or automation is not justified.

## Editorial example — synthetic
`AI can automate a meaningful part of a process, but that does not make it the right first step. The first question is what problem the process is supposed to solve and which decision should improve. If the workflow is still poorly defined, an agent may save time on individual tasks while also reproducing mistakes faster. In practice, the relevant question is not whether the technology can do the work. It is whether the improvement is large enough to justify the cost, complexity and control requirements.`

## Avoid
- generic startup or consulting language;
- `AI is transforming every industry`;
- `unlock`, `revolutionise`, `game-changing`, `next-level` and similar hype;
- legalistic repetition of `shall`, `hereunder`, `pursuant to`, etc.;
- excessive one-line LinkedIn formatting;
- translation-shaped English from Spanish or Catalan.

The target is concise professional explanation, not corporate polish for its own sake.
